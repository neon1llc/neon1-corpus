# Operating principles

A good engagement leaves you better able to run your own technology without me for every small change. These habits get there.

## Begin with the work

The person who does the task shows me the last real example, and I note how often it happens, what goes wrong, and the existing workaround. A request for "AI" sometimes needs a clearer form or a shared folder, and that's a fine answer.

## Leave ownership with you

Accounts, billing, and recovery methods are in your name. I get named, limited access that ends with the work. The handover says who owns each account and how to leave each tool, and I flag hard-to-export tools before you buy.

## Charge for responsibility and useful work

Scoping, testing, documentation, and training are part of the work and the price. A quote covers the whole result, not only time spent changing settings.

## State uncertainty early

I keep apart what we observed, what someone remembers, what we estimated, what a demo showed, and what we propose. Mixing them causes late surprises.

## Match promises to coverage

A system can run around the clock while I work by appointment, so every service says who responds to a failure, and when. An automatic alert isn't a staffed response.

## Prefer the smallest solution that lasts

I check what you already pay for before adding anything, counting maintenance, vendor changes, access, and recovery. Self-hosted free software still costs someone's time.

## Make you an effective participant

I explain choices in terms of your task, show the routine, have you do it, and have you fix one common mistake on purpose. A tool that adds work or watches staff in ways they'd object to counts against itself.

## Make failure recoverable

Before a change, I know how to undo it, recover the data, and keep the business going meanwhile. When a job needs faster response or deeper expertise than mine, I help you find someone.

## Choosing engagements

A project fits when the problem repeats, success can be described, the data is yours to share, the work fits the calendar, and there's a manual fallback. I talk with both the person who pays and the people who use it.

I refer out continuous emergency support, safety-critical equipment, regulated professional judgment, specialist security assurance, and large migrations.

## Commercial basics

- Projects have a written scope, price, and assumptions. The price is fixed when the work is well understood; otherwise it's an estimate, billed for listed hours and never above its high end without your approval.
- Third-party subscriptions, hardware, and travel are separate, and I prefer that vendors bill you directly.
- No extra work or spending happens without your written approval.
- A signed agreement governs each engagement; these principles explain how I work, not replace it.

## Partnerships

Work with other firms follows the [partners](partners.md) standard: who does what is written down before work starts, and any referral fee is disclosed to you.
