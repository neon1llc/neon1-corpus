# Services agreement

> [!warning] An example, not legal advice
> This shows the terms neon1 works under. It's written to be read, not to be clever. Your own counsel should review it before you sign, and the signed copy is the one that counts.

> [!note] Signing in 1NEON
> Clients who work with me in 1NEON sign the [service agreement](../policies/service-agreement.md) there instead, with the blanks filled in: how fixed prices and estimates are billed, a deposit when an option is approved, invoices due within 15 days, and work paused only after 30 days overdue. This blank stays for agreements made outside the portal.

We sign this once, before any paid work. After that, each piece of work is a [proposal](proposal.md): it says what will be done, by when, and for how much. This agreement holds the parts that stay the same from project to project, so a proposal can stay short.

## 1. How work is agreed

1. Each project starts with a proposal that names its work, price, timeline, and the result you'll be able to check.
2. A proposal becomes agreed work when you choose an option in it, by signing it or by approving that option in 1NEON. An approval in 1NEON records who approved, when, and the exact terms shown, and it counts the same as a signature.
3. Agreed work binds both of us: I do the work as described, and you pay as described.
4. If a proposal and this agreement disagree, this agreement wins, unless the proposal names the section it changes.

## 2. What each of us will do

Each proposal lists its own responsibilities. On every project:

| You will | I will |
|---|---|
| Name one person who can make decisions, and answer questions within two business days | Say when I'm working and what I'll do, and answer questions within one business day |
| Give the access the work needs, in your own accounts | Use only that access, keep it confidential, and remove it at handover |
| Make time for testing and training | Test against the agreed checks, show you the result, and train your team |
| Pay for outside accounts and subscriptions directly, in your name | Keep every account, login, and record in your name |

If something outside either of our control holds the work up, I'll pause the timeline, tell you the same day, and give you a new date.

**Systems your business can't run without.** I work in planned appointments on weekday evenings and weekends, with no 24/7 or emergency response. So for anything your business can't run without for a day, I take the work only when someone on your side runs it day to day. That person is named in the [service and recovery profile](../forms/service-and-recovery-profile.md), and the handover is written for them: how it works, how to check it, how to restore it, and whom to call for each part. A [care plan](care-plan.md) sets my response times in writing, within my working hours. A security incident in progress or an outage needs a provider with emergency coverage first.

## 3. Price and payment

1. Each proposal says how it's priced: a fixed fee for the work described, or an estimate billed for the hours worked at a stated rate, up to a stated ceiling.
2. A deposit of ____% is due when you choose an option, and the balance when the work is accepted. Invoices are due within ____ days.
3. Outside costs, such as software, hardware, and subscriptions, are billed to you directly by the vendor. I don't mark them up.
4. If an invoice is more than ____ days late, I may pause work until it's paid, after telling you in writing.

## 4. Changes

Neither of us adds work, cost, or time without a written [change request](../forms/change-request.md) the other approves. Small clarifications that change none of those don't need one.

## 5. Acceptance

The work is accepted when the checks in its proposal pass in front of you. If a check fails, tell me which one within five business days of the demonstration, and I'll fix it and show you again. If I don't hear back in that time, the work is accepted.

## 6. After handover

1. Defects found within 30 days of acceptance are fixed at no charge. A defect is the work not doing what the proposal says.
2. Ongoing care is optional and agreed separately, in a [care plan](care-plan.md).
3. Beyond that, the work is provided as described in its proposal, without other warranties.

## 7. Ownership

1. Once it's paid for, what I make for you is yours: configurations, scripts, documents, and training material.
2. I keep my general know-how and any tools I made before or outside your project. Where your work uses one, you get a permanent license to use it in your business.
3. Open-source and third-party software stays under its own license.

## 8. Confidentiality and data

1. Each of us keeps the other's non-public information confidential and uses it only for the work. This lasts for ____ years after the agreement ends.
2. I work under the [security and data standard](../standards/security-and-data.md). If the work touches health information, a [business associate agreement](business-associate-agreement.md) is signed first. Other personal data may need its own terms.
3. Unless you ask me to delete it, I keep a project's records after it ends, as the privacy policy describes.

## 9. Limits

1. Each of us is responsible for our own work. Neither of us is liable for the other's lost profits or indirect losses.
2. My total liability for a project is limited to the fees paid for that project.
3. These limits don't apply to gross negligence, willful misconduct, or breaking confidentiality.

## 10. Ending

1. Either of us can end this agreement, or a project under it, with ____ days' written notice.
2. On ending, you pay for work done up to that date, and I hand over everything made so far, with its access and records.
3. Sections 7, 8, and 9 continue after it ends.

## 11. General

1. I work as an independent contractor, not an employee.
2. New York law governs this agreement.
3. This agreement and the proposals under it are the whole agreement between us. A change to this agreement needs to be in writing and signed by both of us.
4. Electronic signatures and records count the same as paper ones.
