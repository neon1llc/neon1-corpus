# Writing and client trust

Trust comes from a clear offer, realistic limits, visible work, and always knowing what happens next. The green and the Matrix rain are personality. The useful information should always be easier to find than the decoration.

## How I write

- Concrete statements: what's included, what you own, how a change gets approved, when help is available.
- An unfamiliar technical term is explained once, in terms of your task.
- No "seamless," "guaranteed transformation," or "cutting edge" unless a specific, supported statement can stand in.
- "I" where I'm personally doing the work, and "neon1" for the business. I don't imply a staff or a partner network that doesn't exist.
- Warmth shows in the explanation and the follow-through, not in sentimental reassurance.

## Documents that help you act

Documents use a clear heading structure, comfortable spacing, and real tables only for real comparisons. Policies stay in prose. Long manuals get contents and page numbers. Every downloadable document carries its title, edition, and date.

Each kind of document puts its key facts where you'll look for them:

| Document | What should be easy to find |
|---|---|
| Proposal | Your problem, the deliverables, price, assumptions, next step |
| Case study | Scope, my role, evidence, limits |
| Standard or manual | The rule, the procedure, the evidence |
| Form | What to fill in, and what an empty field means |

## Evidence before claims

Every public claim has a record: the wording, what supports it, its scope, the date, permission, and its limits. A case study states the problem, my actual role, what was delivered, how acceptance was checked, the result, and what's still unknown. A clear result without a number beats an invented percentage.

Work I did as an employee can show my background. It never becomes neon1's client work by implication. Testimonials reflect the person's real experience, disclose relationships such as family, and never put a technical guarantee in a client's mouth, following the [FTC's guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers). Experiments are labeled as experiments, so nobody mistakes one for a supported product.

## The website

The first screen says who neon1 helps, with what, and how to get in touch. Navigation is ordinary and visible, text is readable, and the layout works on a phone. The terminal and the white rabbit are optional easter eggs. Nobody has to type a command, wait for an animation, or know a film to reach the offer.

The site uses semantic headings, descriptive links, visible keyboard focus, labeled controls, clear errors, enough contrast, a pause control for motion, and a calm version for people who prefer reduced motion. I aim for [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) AA and test the actual pages.

## Helping you choose

I show a concrete example, explain the tradeoff, and offer a sensible next step: a free first call, a paid assessment, or a scoped project. When an existing tool or another provider would serve you better, I say so. The [Alternatives shelf](../landscape/where-else-to-get-help.md) exists for exactly that. No manufactured urgency, ever.

## The brand

neon1 is written in lowercase, even at the start of a sentence. The company is neon1 LLC. The green is #035704 in documents. Body text uses Aptos where available and Inter elsewhere, with JetBrains Mono for code and labels. The logo isn't redrawn, stretched, or recolored.
