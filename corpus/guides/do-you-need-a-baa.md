# Do you need a business associate agreement?

Under HIPAA, a practice can share patient information with a vendor working for it only after the vendor signs a business associate agreement (BAA). The BAA says how the vendor will protect the information and what it will do if something goes wrong.

## Who needs one

You need a BAA with a vendor when **both** are true:

- [ ] Your practice is a covered entity, such as a provider that bills insurance electronically, a health plan, or a clearinghouse
- [ ] The vendor creates, receives, keeps, or sends patient information for you

Examples: your practice management or health record system, a billing service, a cloud storage or email provider holding patient messages, an IT consultant who works inside those systems, and a transcription service.

## Who usually doesn't

- A vendor that never touches patient information, such as someone fixing your printer with no access to records
- Your internet provider, if it only carries data
- Other providers you share information with for treatment, who have their own HIPAA duties

## Before you sign

- **Is patient information needed at all?** Often made-up or de-identified data will do.
- **What's the smallest amount needed?** Ask the vendor to name it.
- **How fast will they report a problem?** Look for a number of days.
- **Who else will see it?** Subcontractors must agree to the same terms.
- **What happens at the end?** The data is returned or destroyed, confirmed in writing.
- **Do their tools have BAAs?** If they put your data into another service, such as an AI tool or form builder, that service needs one too.
