# Do you need a business associate agreement?

> [!warning] General information, not legal advice
> This guide explains the idea in plain words. Your privacy officer or counsel makes the call for your practice.

Under HIPAA, a health practice can share patient information with a vendor working on its behalf only after the vendor signs a business associate agreement (BAA), saying how it will protect the information and what it will do if something goes wrong.

## Who needs one

You need a BAA with a vendor when **both** are true:

- [ ] Your practice is a covered entity, such as a provider that bills insurance electronically, a health plan, or a clearinghouse
- [ ] The vendor creates, receives, keeps, or sends patient information for you

Common examples: your practice management or electronic health record system, a billing service, a cloud storage or email provider holding patient messages, an IT consultant who works inside those systems, and a transcription service.

## Who usually doesn't

- A vendor that never touches patient information, such as someone fixing your printer with no access to records
- Your internet provider just carrying data, without storing it
- Other providers you share information with for treatment, who have their own HIPAA duties

## Before you sign

- **Is patient information needed at all?** Often the work can be done with made-up or de-identified data instead.
- **What's the smallest amount needed?** Ask the vendor to name it.
- **How fast will they report a problem?** Look for a specific number of days.
- **Who else will see it?** Subcontractors must agree to the same terms.
- **What happens at the end?** The data should be returned or destroyed, and confirmed in writing.
- **Do their own tools have BAAs?** If they put your data into another service, such as an AI tool or a form builder, that service needs one too.
