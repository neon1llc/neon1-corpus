# Why I started neon1

The people who most need good technical help are the least likely to have any. A company of a few hundred people has an IT department. A three-person tax office, a dental practice with two chairs, or a bakery on a busy block has the owner, who is also the bookkeeper and the person who restarts the router when the card reader drops off the Wi-Fi on a Saturday morning. The SBA [counts more than 36.2 million](https://www.sba.gov/Saturday) independent businesses in the country, and most of them run that way.

I'm Pradeep. For more than eight years I've built and run cloud systems as an engineer, and I've been the person people call when their technology stops cooperating. The fix was rarely the hard part. Nobody had explained the problem in words that made sense, so the same thing broke again, or the owner paid for something much bigger than they needed because a bigger thing was all anyone offered.

neon1 is my answer. I take on [four kinds of work](../engagement/index.md): workflow automation, cloud and AI engineering, networks and connected systems, and security readiness, for businesses, technical teams, and individuals. I work remotely across the US, and on site within about 30 miles of Queens. Outside the US, ask.

Every project runs through [five steps](../engagement/process.md): listen, propose, decide, build, and hand over. I learn how the work gets done today before I suggest anything. Then you get one recommendation, or options when there's a real choice, and you decide. Sometimes the right option is a setting, a cleanup, or leaving something alone. When we're done, you have the documentation and the accounts, and everything stays yours.

This blog will carry about one post a week: answers to questions small offices ask, like [what a federal security rule means for a tax office](tax-preparers-are-financial-institutions.md), why I run neon1 the way I do, and what building neon1 in the open has taught me.

## Starting is now free

An owner who runs the shop all day doesn't have 15 minutes at two in the afternoon. They have them at ten at night, after closing. So anyone can now open a [free 1NEON account](../guides/free-accounts.md), the portal where I run every client project, and outline what their projects need in their own words. From the account you can ask for an assessment of up to three projects, or book a call, and I'll have read what you wrote before we talk.

The account holds up to 3 projects. Inviting your team, approvals, and billing open once we agree to work together. An account left unchanged for 30 days is removed after a few reminders. To see the portal first, the [guided walkthrough](https://neon1.tech/1NEON/live) plays a made-up project in about three minutes.

Small Business Saturday is November 28. If something about your technology has been bothering you this busy season, write it down tonight.
