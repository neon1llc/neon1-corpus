# Services agreement

> [!warning] An example, not legal advice
> This shows the terms neon1 works under. Your own counsel should review it before you sign, and the signed copy is the one that counts.

We sign this once, before any paid work. After that, each piece of work is a [proposal](proposal.md) saying what will be done, by when, and for how much. This agreement holds the parts that stay the same, so a proposal can stay short.

**Client (legal name):** ____ **Mailing address:** ____

**neon1 LLC**, 418 Broadway, Ste R, Albany, NY 12207

**Effective date:** ____

## 1. How work is agreed

1. Each project starts with a proposal that names its services and what each is worth, the rate or price, the billing schedule, the timeline, and the result you'll be able to check.
2. A proposal becomes agreed work when you choose an option in it, by signing it.
3. Agreed work binds both of us: I do the work as described, and you pay as described.
4. If a proposal and this agreement disagree, this agreement wins, unless the proposal names the section it changes.

## 2. What each of us will do

Each proposal lists its own responsibilities. On every project:

| You will | I will |
|---|---|
| Name one person who can make decisions, and answer questions within two business days | Say when I'm working and what I'll do, and answer questions in my working hours (Monday to Thursday 6pm to 9pm, Friday 6pm to 8pm, and weekends 8am to 8pm, Eastern time), usually within a day |
| Give the access the work needs, in your own accounts | Use only that access, keep it confidential, and remove it at handover |
| Make time for testing and training | Test against the agreed checks, show you the result, and train your team |
| Buy outside accounts and subscriptions in your name, unless a proposal says I buy them for you | Keep every account, login, and record in your name |

If something outside either of our control holds the work up, I'll pause the timeline, tell you the same day, and give you a new date.

**Systems your business can't run without.** I work in planned appointments on weekday evenings and weekends, with no 24/7 or emergency response. So for anything your business can't run without for a day, I take the work only when someone on your side runs it day to day. That person is named in the [service and recovery profile](../forms/service-and-recovery-profile.md), and the handover is written for them: how it works, how to check it, how to restore it, and whom to call for each part. A [care plan](care-plan.md) sets my response times in writing, within my working hours. A security incident in progress or an outage needs a provider with emergency coverage first.

## 3. Price and payment

1. Each proposal prices the work as an estimate that discovery sets: hours worked at a stated rate, up to a stated high end, billed every two weeks unless the proposal says otherwise. Unforeseen work or purchases are agreed with you before I add them. A proposal may instead state a fixed fee for a small, well-defined job, billed by its milestones or on delivery.
2. A deposit of 30% of the fixed fee or of the estimate's low end is due when you choose an option, unless the proposal states another share. It counts toward later invoices, and I refund any part the work done doesn't use.
3. I send each invoice within 7 days after the period, milestone, or delivery it bills. Invoices are due within 15 days unless we agree otherwise in writing.
4. Payment for properly performed work doesn't depend on you recording acceptance (section 5), and paying an invoice doesn't mean you accept the work or give up a valid claim.
5. If you believe an invoice includes work that wasn't agreed, performed, or delivered as required, tell me which part you dispute and why, and we'll work to resolve it. The undisputed amount stays payable by its original due date.
6. Outside costs, such as software, hardware, and subscriptions, are billed to you directly by the vendor. When a proposal says I buy something for you, you pay me back exactly what I paid, shown by the receipt, with no markup.
7. I send a reminder the business day after an invoice is due. If an undisputed amount is more than 7 days overdue, I may pause work until it's paid, after telling you in writing. A pause never disables your systems, withholds your credentials or data, or deletes anything of yours.

## 4. Changes

Neither of us adds work, cost, or time without a written [change request](../forms/change-request.md) the other approves. Small clarifications that change none of those don't need one.

## 5. Review and acceptance

1. When the work is ready, I hand it over: the agreed deliverables, the documentation, and the access you need to use and review it. I record that date and tell you in writing.
2. Please review the work within 10 business days, and record your acceptance, in 1NEON or in writing, or tell me which agreed check doesn't pass. If a check fails, I correct the work and present it again. Silence never counts as acceptance.
3. A delay in your review doesn't postpone payment for properly performed work or change the payment schedule.
4. If I can't continue because I'm waiting for your access, information, decisions, or participation, I tell you what's needed and how the delay affects the work. After 10 business days from that notice, I may pause the affected work and adjust its schedule. After 30 calendar days, I may end the affected project under section 11. You remain responsible for work properly performed and purchases you approved before then, and I don't charge for work not yet done because of a delay.

## 6. After handover

1. Defects reported within 30 days of handover are fixed at no charge, and stay mine to fix until resolved. A defect is the work not doing what the proposal says. Marking a project complete doesn't start the 30 days; handover has to happen.
2. Ongoing care is optional and agreed separately, in a [care plan](care-plan.md).
3. Beyond that, the work is provided as described in its proposal, without other warranties, except rights the law doesn't allow to be limited.

## 7. Ownership

1. Once it's paid for, what I make for you is yours: configurations, scripts, documents, and training material. Until then, you may use it in your business as the work intends.
2. I keep my general know-how and any tools I made before or outside your project. Where your work uses one, you get a permanent license to use it in your business.
3. Open-source and third-party software stays under its own license.
4. Your existing systems, data, accounts, credentials, and records are always yours, whatever is owed.

## 8. Confidentiality and data

1. Each of us keeps the other's non-public information confidential and uses it only for the work. This lasts for ____ years after the agreement ends.
2. I work under the [security and data standard](../standards/security-and-data.md). If the work touches health information, a [business associate agreement](business-associate-agreement.md) is signed first. Other personal data may need its own terms.
3. Unless you ask me to delete it, I keep a project's records after it ends, as the privacy policy describes. I keep signed agreements, approvals, invoices, and payment records for seven years as my business practice, and longer while an actual or reasonably expected dispute, investigation, or legal obligation needs them.

## 9. Showing the work

1. Anonymized case studies, articles, and examples of the work (choose one; nothing is pre-marked): ☐ yes ☐ decide later: ask me about each project before publication ☐ no. Your choice doesn't affect the services or their price.
2. Anonymized means neither the information itself nor its combination with other reasonably available information can reasonably identify you, your people, or your customers. I never publish your confidential information, personal information, security-sensitive details, or identifiable records. Section 8 allows what you choose here.
3. Listing your name as a client (choose one): ☐ ask me first ☐ yes ☐ no.
4. Your name, logo or trademarks, quotations or testimonials, endorsements, identifiable screenshots, and linking your name to a particular case study each take a separate yes. Permission for anonymized case studies doesn't cover them.
5. You can change these choices in writing at any time, from then on. If you withdraw permission, I stop further use and remove the affected material from the places I control. I can't guarantee removal of independent copies or archives kept by others.

## 10. Limits

1. Each of us is responsible for our own work. Neither of us is liable for the other's lost profits or indirect losses.
2. My total liability for all claims about a project is limited to the fees paid and payable for that project.
3. These limits don't apply to gross negligence, willful misconduct, breaking confidentiality, or what the law doesn't allow to be limited, and they never reduce what you owe for work properly performed and purchases you approved.

## 11. Ending

1. Either of us can end this agreement, or a project under it, with 14 days' written notice.
2. On ending, you pay for work properly performed and purchases you approved up to that date, not the price of work not yet done, and I refund any unearned deposit. I return your access and credentials and hand over everything made so far, with its records, which becomes yours once it's paid for.
3. Sections 3 (for amounts owed), 7, 8, 9, and 10 continue after it ends.

## 12. General

1. I work as an independent contractor, not an employee.
2. We'll first try to settle a disagreement by talking it through. New York law governs this agreement, and disputes go to the state or federal courts in New York County, New York. Nothing here waives rights the law doesn't allow to be waived.
3. This agreement and the proposals under it are the whole agreement between us. A change to this agreement needs to be in writing and signed by both of us.
4. Electronic signatures and records count the same as paper ones. Each person signing confirms they are authorized to sign for their side, and each of us keeps a copy of the exact signed text.
