# How I use AI

*Last updated November 9, 2026.*

I use AI tools in my own work, mainly for research and review. This page says where I use them, where I don't, and what stays my responsibility.

## Where I use it

- **Research.** Gathering and condensing sources, standards, and options on a topic, so I can review them and decide what to do next.
- **Understanding a need.** Organizing what I learn in discovery into a clearer picture, and checking whether I've missed an angle.
- **Reviewing writing.** Checking documents, including the ones in this Knowledge Center, for gaps, unclear wording, and mistakes.
- **Reviewing code.** Looking for bugs, security issues, and missing tests before a change is merged. Changes go through named branches and automated checks, and once a project reaches version 1.0.0, through protected branches that require approval.

## What stays with me

- AI output is one input to my judgment. The decisions are mine.
- Everything published or shipped under neon1's name is reviewed by me, and I'm accountable for it.
- Facts, figures, and sources are checked against the original before they're used.

## Your information

- Your confidential information, passwords, and payment details don't go into an AI service unless you've approved that service in writing.
- Health information goes into an AI service only if you've approved it and the service is covered by a business associate agreement.
- Many projects don't need your data in an AI tool at all, and I set them up that way when I can.

## AI in what I build for you

AI features inside a system I build for you are a separate matter. They're tested and released under the [AI quality and oversight](../standards/ai-quality.md) standard. Neo, the chat on neon1.tech, is an automated assistant and says so.

Ask me at [hello@neon1.tech](mailto:hello@neon1.tech) if you'd like to know how AI was or wasn't used in your project.
