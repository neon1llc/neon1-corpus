# Where else to get help

neon1 isn't the right fit for everyone, and I'd rather you end up with the right help than with me. Here are the main alternatives, how they usually work, and where each one is honestly better. Prices change, so check each provider's own site for current figures.

## At a glance

| Option | Best for | Usually charges | Where it beats neon1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doing it yourself, with vendor support | One clear task in a tool you already use | Included in your subscription | Free, immediate, and the vendor knows its own product best |
| Free advising (SCORE, Small Business Development Centers, libraries) | Planning, first steps, a second opinion | Free | Free, unbiased, and broader than technology |
| Freelance marketplaces | A well-defined, one-off build | Hourly or fixed price, plus platform fees | Huge choice and quick start for narrow tasks |
| Managed service providers (MSPs) | Ongoing help desk, devices, and security for a team | A monthly fee per user or per device | Round-the-clock coverage, staff depth, and accountability for day-to-day IT |
| Retail and local repair shops | A broken device or a home setup | Flat fee per job, sometimes a membership | Walk-in convenience and parts on hand |
| Automation and software agencies | A larger build with a team behind it | Project fees or retainers | More people, more capacity, faster on big projects |
| Fractional CTO or IT director | Technology strategy and leadership for a growing company | A monthly retainer | Senior leadership presence over months, managing vendors and staff |
| Large consultancies | Enterprise transformation | Large project contracts | Scale, industry depth, and formal assurance services |
| Security specialists (MSSPs, auditors, penetration testers) | Monitoring, audits, formal testing | Retainers or fixed engagements | Formal assurance and 24/7 security monitoring neon1 doesn't offer |

## Doing it yourself, with vendor support

Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, QuickBooks, Square, and most other business tools have built-in automation, templates, and support included in what you already pay. Automation tools such as Zapier and Make have libraries of ready-made connections.

**Better than neon1 when** the task lives inside one product and its documentation covers it. Try it first. If you get stuck or the workaround starts to sprawl across several tools, that's usually when outside help pays off.

## Free advising

[SCORE](https://www.score.org/how-mentoring-works/) offers free one-on-one mentoring to any owner of a U.S.-based business, and New York's [Small Business Development Centers](https://www.nysbdc.org/) offer free one-on-one advising funded by the U.S. Small Business Administration and the state. Public libraries, such as the [New York Public Library](https://www.nypl.org/), run small business programs too.

**Better than neon1 when** you're still deciding what to do, need business planning beyond technology, or want an unbiased second opinion before spending anything. They generally advise rather than build.

## Freelance marketplaces

Platforms such as [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/) and [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/) list thousands of freelancers. On its [client pricing page](https://www.upwork.com/pricing/client), Upwork charges clients a 5% service fee on its Basic plan or 10% on Business Plus, plus a contract initiation fee. Fiverr adds a service fee at checkout that you see before you pay.

**Better than neon1 when** the task is narrow and well defined, such as "build this form" or "fix this spreadsheet formula," and you're comfortable writing the specification and checking the result. **Watch for:** quality varies widely, and handover, documentation, and account ownership depend entirely on who you pick. Make sure accounts are created in your name.

## Managed service providers

An MSP takes over day-to-day IT for a monthly fee, usually per user or per device: help desk, device management, backups, security tools, and often after-hours response. Industry pricing guides put typical small-business plans somewhere in the low hundreds of dollars per user per month, with minimums for very small teams. Get a written quote.

**Better than neon1 when** you have staff who need a help desk, you need someone to answer at 2 a.m., or you want one company accountable for every device. neon1 works by appointment and doesn't run a help desk. **Watch for:** long contracts, tools and accounts held in the MSP's name, and exit fees. Ask how you'd leave before you sign.

## Retail and local repair shops

Big-box tech services such as Best Buy's [Geek Squad](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/electronics/services/pcmcat1528819595254.c?id=pcmcat1528819595254) and independent repair shops handle broken devices, in-home installs, and setup, usually at a price per job. Best Buy's paid Total membership adds 24/7 tech support and protection plans for what you buy there.

**Better than neon1 when** a device is broken, needs parts, or you want walk-in convenience and a store's warranty. **Watch for:** they fix the device in front of them. They rarely look at how your tools work together.

## Automation and software agencies

Agencies bring a team: designers, developers, and a project manager. They suit larger builds with deadlines that one person can't meet.

**Better than neon1 when** the project needs several people working at once, or needs a dedicated team for months. neon1 takes at most two clients at a time.

## Fractional CTO or IT director

A part-time senior technology leader on a retainer, often for growing companies that need a technology strategy, vendor management, or a hiring plan.

**Better than neon1 when** you need a leader in your meetings every week over many months, managing staff and vendors on your behalf.

## Large consultancies

Firms such as Accenture, Deloitte, and their peers run enterprise-scale programs.

**Better than neon1 when** you're a large organization needing scale, formal methodology, industry specialists, or audit and assurance services. For a small business they're usually the wrong size.

## Security specialists

Managed security service providers watch systems around the clock. Auditors issue formal opinions, such as SOC 2 reports, and penetration testers try to break in under contract.

**Better than neon1 when** you need formal assurance, an audit opinion, a penetration test report, or 24/7 security monitoring. neon1 helps you get ready for an audit and fixes the basics, but doesn't issue opinions or run a security operations center. I'll refer you.

## Where neon1 fits

neon1 suits owner-run businesses, small practices, and households that want their tools to work together, want to understand and own the result, and value someone who listens first and leaves good documentation behind. It's the wrong choice if you need 24/7 coverage, a team of many, formal assurance, or someone to make regulated professional judgments.

If none of this fits, ask anyway. If I'm not the right person, I'll try to point you to someone who is.
