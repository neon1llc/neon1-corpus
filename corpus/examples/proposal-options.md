## Discovery

**How it works today**

- Orders arrive by email and voicemail. Every night after close, Maria and Sam retype them into a spreadsheet. It takes about an hour.
- Last Friday had 41 orders: 35 emails and 6 voicemails, typed in from 8 to 9pm.
- About three orders a week are missed or typed wrong, and someone finds out at pickup.
- Everything runs on the bakery's Google account. Maria does most of it from her phone.

**What you asked for**

- Save time and stop mistakes on the nightly order list, within about a month
- Train Sam on whatever we set up, before the holiday rush

## Objectives

**The result:** every order lands in one list on its own, and nobody retypes it.

**How we'll know it worked,** measured the same way for two weeks before and after:

- Minutes retyping each night: from 60 to under 10
- Missed or wrong orders a week: from 3 to 0

**Requirements**

- Nothing is retyped twice (must have)
- Works on a phone (must have)
- Running before the holiday rush (should have)

## Options

### A. Quick fix

An order form that writes straight into your order spreadsheet, and an email reply that points customers to it.

**Includes**

- A Google Form with your items, quantities, pickup day and time, name, phone, and notes
- Each order lands as a row in your spreadsheet, sorted by pickup
- An automatic reply to order emails with the form's link, and the link on your website and Google profile
- Five test orders with you, then a 15-minute walkthrough for you and Sam

**Not included:** orders that still come by email or voicemail, which are typed in as today, and a daily summary.

**Target result:** form orders appear in the spreadsheet within a minute, with nothing retyped.

**neon1's time:** about 3 hours **Timeline:** 2 days **Paid directly by you:** nothing. It uses your Google account.

### B. Solid setup (recommended)

The form from A, plus order emails and voicemails added to the same list on their own, and a summary at 7pm.

**Includes**

- Everything in A
- Order emails and voicemail transcripts copied into the list as they arrive, each marked to check, so nothing is retyped or missed
- A 7pm email to you and Sam: tomorrow's pickups, and anything still to check
- A dry run on last week's 41 real orders before go-live
- A week side by side with the old way, with two short check-ins
- A one-page runbook and a 30-minute walkthrough, recorded for new staff

**Not included:** taking payment online, and changes to your phone service.

**Depends on:** your phone service emailing voicemail transcripts. Most do, and if yours doesn't, a free Google Voice number can.

**Target result:** every order is in one list by 7pm, with under 10 minutes of checking a night.

**neon1's time:** about 10 hours **Timeline:** 2 weeks **Paid directly by you:** nothing. It uses your Google account.

### C. Fuller build

Everything in B, plus an online order page where customers choose a pickup time, pay ahead, and get a reminder.

**Includes**

- Everything in B
- An order page on Square's free online store, with your menu, pickup times, and holiday cutoffs
- Payment when ordering, and an email or text reminder the day before pickup
- Online orders copied into the same list

**Not included:** delivery, and photography. I set up the photos you send.

**Depends on:** your menu with prices, a week before go-live.

**Target result:** most orders arrive paid, with a pickup time, and never pass through the inbox.

**neon1's time:** about 17 hours **Timeline:** 3 weeks **Paid directly by you:** about $20 a month for the link to your spreadsheet, plus Square's card fees on online orders.

### Which to choose

- Choose **A** for the fastest, smallest fix, if most customers will use a form.
- Choose **B** to stop the retyping without asking customers to change how they order.
- Choose **C** if you also want orders paid ahead, and can spare a few hours setting up the menu.
