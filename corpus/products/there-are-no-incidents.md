# Incident response for small teams

*Public · v0.1.1*

**Plan for a security incident, practice it, and handle a real one.** Open on [GitHub](https://github.com/neon1llc/incident-response-for-small-teams) for owners, office managers, IT providers, and trainers at businesses with 2 to 50 people, especially those holding client financial, tax, or health information.

- **The runbook:** get ready, decide whether it's an incident (with four severity levels), the first hour, contain, investigate, notify, recover, and learn, following NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3.
- **Playbooks** for the six incidents small businesses see most: business email compromise, ransomware, a lost or stolen device, a compromised account, a vendor breach, and client data sent to the wrong person.
- **Tabletop exercises:** a facilitator guide and six 60 to 90 minute exercises, one per playbook, with staged injects, questions, and what good answers sound like.
- **Templates:** a one-page response plan, a contact sheet, an incident log, a notification worksheet, an after-action report, and a tabletop record.
- **Notification rules:** example deadlines from named U.S. federal and New York rules, including the FTC Safeguards Rule, HIPAA, Regulation S-P, 23 NYCRR 500, and New York's 30-day breach notice, each linked to the rule's text.
- **Licenses:** written material under CC BY-SA 4.0, code under MIT, and the names reserved.

## Current limits

Notification rules cover U.S. federal requirements and New York. Other states are linked through the NCSL list rather than summarized. Notification requirements vary by jurisdiction, sector, and contract, and a lawyer decides what applies. It's training, not legal advice.
